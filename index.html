How to play

You are approach and tower at . Arrivals (magenta routes) enter on STARs and must be descended and cleared for an approach. Departures wait at the holding point: give them their clearance (caf), clear them for take-off (cto), climb them, and hand them off (ho) once they're above FL100 and 20 nm out. They follow their SID (blue route).

Keep 3 nm or 1000 ft between airborne aircraft, and on final keep 4 nm behind a heavy (H) for another heavy and 5 nm for a medium. Yellow tags = predicted conflict or wake spacing, red = separation lost.

Ground chart

Switch to Ground chart (tab at the top left of the scope, or .gnd). It shows a detailed aerodrome chart: runways with threshold, touchdown-zone and aiming-point markings, approach lights, PAPIs and ILS antennas; taxiways with curved junction fillets; runway holding positions with stop bars and mandatory signs; stands with lead-in lines and jet bridges; the terminal, tower, fire station and cargo apron.

Departures start on a stand and call Delivery. Give them caf, then assign the taxi route: select the aircraft, click the taxiways in order, then click the runway holding point and press Send taxi. Or type it, e.g. taxi A7 via P J3 A at Ravenwood or taxi V5 via A G V at Manchester. They push back in an arc (choose the facing with pb e / pb w), then taxi along the curves on the chart, slowing for corners and stopping behind traffic. At the runway holding point Ground hands them to Tower automatically. Tower's lu / cto taxis them onto the runway (the stop bar goes off and the green lead-on lights come on) and, with a take-off clearance, rolls as soon as they are aligned.

Arrivals brake for the first exit they can make (rapid exits are taken at speed). Once clear of the runway, Tower hands them to Ground automatically and they ask for taxi: taxi sends them to their stand (taxi 14 for stand 14). hpos / cont stop and restart a taxiing aircraft. Right-click an aircraft on the chart for its instructions, or a holding point / stand for taxi options. With Auto taxi ticked, ground movement is handled for you.

Emergencies

Rarely, an aircraft has an emergency. It calls MAYDAY or PAN, squawks 7700 (7600 for a radio failure), and a banner appears on the scope. Acknowledge it with ack (this alerts the emergency services), then give it priority: the shortest safe route to the runway, other traffic kept out of its way or held.

Engine failure (MAYDAY)After take-off: stops climbing around 3000–4000 ft with poor performance and asks for vectors back to land. It may stop on the runway after landing.
Bird strike (PAN)After take-off: returns to land.
Medical (PAN)Arrival asks for a priority approach; a quick landing earns a bonus.
Minimum fuel (MAYDAY)Cannot hold. Land it before its fuel endurance runs out, or it diverts.
Depressurisation (MAYDAY)Emergency descent to FL100 with a turn off track. Watch for traffic below it.
Hydraulic failure (PAN)Faster, longer final, and it stops on the runway, closing it for about 5 minutes until it is towed clear.
Radio failure (7600)No replies. It follows its route, flies the approach and lands. Keep everything else clear of it.

Set how often they happen in the toolbar, or practise with .emg engine, .emg fuel and so on.

EuroScope-style scope

Data tags show callsign, Mode-C level ↑↓ cleared level, ground speed/10, heading or next fix, and assigned speed. Click a tag field to open its list: the cleared level, the heading/direct-to field (with turn direction), or the speed. Click the callsign for the aircraft menu (transfer, approach clearances, hold, go-around, landing clearance). Tags of aircraft handed to you by another controller flash until you assume them (click the callsign or asm); aircraft you hand away briefly show →TWR etc. Shift+drag (or middle-drag) between points or aircraft draws a range/bearing line, with the predicted minimum distance between two aircraft; right-click a line to remove it. The toolbar sets the speed-vector length, history dots and the map, areas and airspace layers. The METAR is shown in the top bar.

Mouse

Click an aircraft to select it. Press and hold a waypoint to send the selected aircraft direct to it (a ring fills around the fix). Right-click a waypoint for direct, holding (as published or with your own turns) and approach options. Right-click empty map to turn the aircraft towards that point. The coloured line ahead of each aircraft is its predicted flight path, including turns, holds and the localizer intercept; a dashed line means it is on a heading (vectors).

Realism

Wind drifts aircraft that are flying a heading; on routes and the localizer the pilots correct for it. Turns are standard rate (3°/s) limited to 25° of bank, so fast aircraft turn wider. Holds are flown with proper direct, parallel and teardrop entries, 1-minute legs up to FL140 (1.5 min above), at holding speed (230 kt). Speed is limited to 250 kt below FL100. Transition altitude is ft; pilots set QNH when descending below it. Published holds are marked with a dot under the fix; hover a fix with an aircraft selected, or tick Procs, to see them.

Positions

Pick your position in the top bar (or .pos gnd / .pos twr / .pos app / .pos all). AI controllers work the other positions, and you can switch at any time.

GroundDepartures: clearance (caf), pushback, taxi to the holding point on the route you give, then transfer to Tower (ho). Arrivals: Tower transfers them to you after they vacate. Taxi them to their stand. Opens on the ground chart.
TowerDepartures arrive at the holding point from Ground: lu, cto, then transfer to Departure (ho) passing about 1500–5000 ft. Arrivals: Approach transfers them to you on the ILS. Give cl (cleared to land) or they go around at 1 nm, then transfer them to Ground (ho) once they vacate. An arrival reaching the threshold with the runway occupied goes around.
ApproachArrivals from the entry fix: descend, sequence, vector or clear the approach, then transfer to Tower (ho) once on the ILS. Departures check in after Tower transfers them: climb them and hand them to Control (ho) above FL100, 20 nm out.
CombinedYou do everything. Frequency changes between your positions and landing clearances are automatic, and Auto taxi can do the taxiing.

Aircraft on the AI's frequency have grey tags and strips. Their radio calls appear dimmed in the log. You only lose points for separation on aircraft that are on your frequency.

Type a callsign (or just its digits) followed by commands, or select an aircraft and type commands only. Several commands can go on one line: BAW123 tl 180 d 4000 s 220.

Scoring

Landing +100 · handoff to Control +100 (early +50/+20) · Tower→Departure +30 · Approach→Tower +20 · go-around −10 · runway not clear −50 · separation loss −50 · wake spacing −30 · departure leaving without handoff −50 · arrival lost −100.

Tip: arrivals descend on their own only after app RWY. With i RWY you must vector them onto the localizer (≈30° intercept) and descend them to 3000–4000 ft first — the glideslope is captured from below.
